Map Data Sources & Methodology

This page explains where each map layer's data comes from, how it was derived, and what limitations apply. Layers marked ESTIMATED are geographic approximations and should not be treated as authoritative.

✈ Airports, Airstrips & Heliports VERIFIED DATA
Primary source
OurAirports (ourairports.com/data/) — open dataset of world airports, updated continuously by a community of aviation enthusiasts and cross-referenced against official national AIPs. Downloaded June 2026 for England.
Coverage
1,214 airfields in England, classified into three categories: Airports (commercial and military scheduled/charter), Airstrips (private and grass strips, glider fields, ultralights), and Heliports (all rotary-wing facilities). Each record carries usage tags (commercial / military / private) and an active/inactive flag.
Distance calculation
Haversine formula — great-circle distance in miles between property coordinates and the nearest facility in each category. Computed at build time for every property and stored in the results JSON. The map circle radius slider lets you filter by these distances in real time.
Limitations
OurAirports data is community-maintained and may be incomplete for very small unlicensed strips. Inactive sites are included because their runways can still be reinstated or used informally. Flight paths are not necessarily towards the nearest facility.
⚠️ Military Danger Areas VERIFIED DATA
Primary source
UK AIP (Aeronautical Information Publication) published by NATS on behalf of the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA). Danger area boundaries are defined in ENR 5.1 of the AIP and are the definitive legal source for UK airspace restrictions. Cross-referenced with MOD published training area boundaries.
Coverage
22 danger areas covering England, selected for their relevance to low-level noise impact on residential areas. Includes EG D323 Spadeadam, EG D201 Donna Nook, EG D307 Holbeach, EG D208 Salisbury Plain, EG D108/109 Dartmoor, and others.
What they mean
Danger areas are volumes of airspace where activities hazardous to aircraft occur — live weapons firing, low-level fast jet manoeuvres, rocket firing, drone swarm exercises. Properties near active danger areas experience military aircraft at very low altitudes (sometimes below 250ft AGL) on a regular basis.
Polygon accuracy
Polygon coordinates were hand-digitised from the AIP boundary descriptions and MOD maps. Accuracy is typically within 0.5–1 mile of the published boundary. The AIP is the authoritative source; these polygons are for indicative purposes only.
🛡 Military Aerodrome Traffic Zones (MATZ) VERIFIED DATA
Primary source
UK AIP ENR 2.2 — Military Aerodrome Traffic Zones. Each MATZ centre point and associated airfield was taken directly from the AIP table, which lists all permanently established MATZs with their central coordinates.
Coverage
23 MATZs in England, including Coningsby, Marham, Lossiemouth (Scotland, not shown), Waddington, Lakenheath, Mildenhall, Cottesmore (Wittering), Benson, Brize Norton, Odiham, Yeovilton, Culdrose, St Mawgan, Chivenor, Merryfield, Valley (Wales, not shown), Shawbury, Church Fenton, Linton-on-Ouse, Topcliffe, Leeming, Leconfield, and Dishforth.
How zones are drawn
A standard MATZ consists of a 5 nautical mile (5.75 mi) radius circle centred on the airfield, extending from surface to 3,000ft AMSL. Some MATZs include a rectangular stub aligned with the instrument approach. The map shows the 5nm circle only; the stub is omitted for clarity.
What they mean
Within a MATZ, military ATC has priority. High volumes of military circuit traffic (touch-and-go approaches, formation flying, low-level breaks) occur daily. Properties inside a MATZ circle are very likely to be directly under approach/departure paths.
🔻 Low Flying Areas (LFA) VERIFIED DATA
Primary source
UK AIP ENR 5.6 — Low Flying. Published LFA boundaries are described in the AIP with reference coordinates. The MOD Low Flying Programme leaflet provides additional context on the LFA system.
Coverage
10 LFAs relevant to England: LFA7 (Wales/Borders), LFA1 (Scottish Highlands, shown for northern England reference), and the main English LFAs covering the Peak District, Pennines, North York Moors, Lake District, East Anglian coast, and Dartmoor/Exmoor.
What they mean
LFAs are designated regions where military aircraft routinely fly below 2,000ft AGL (and often below 500ft or 250ft). The UK Low Flying Programme schedules approximately 40,000–55,000 low-level sorties per year across all UK LFAs. Properties within LFAs can expect military jets or helicopters at low altitude on most weekdays during the training season.
Daily sortie estimates
Each LFA popup shows an estimated range of daily low-level sorties (below 10,000 ft) across the whole area. These are derived from:
  • MOD Annual Low Flying Statistics — published as HC Written Answers to Parliamentary Questions (typically tabled annually, most recently covering 2022–23). These give total sorties per LFA per year, from which daily averages are computed.
  • MOD Low Flying Statistics collection — gov.uk/government/collections/military-low-flying-statistics — annual statistical releases since 2010.
  • Station-specific sources where MOD figures are unavailable (notably USAF operations in LFA3): 48th Fighter Wing public affairs releases, USAF EUCOM posture statements, and RAF Shawbury/Culdrose/Yeovilton published flying hour reports.

These are area-wide figures. Any individual location within an LFA will typically see a fraction of the total — concentrated along valleys, coast approaches, and known training routes rather than distributed evenly.

Polygon accuracy
LFA polygons are based on published AIP boundary descriptions and have been refined to up to 8 vertices per boundary following geographic features (national park edges, valley systems, coastlines). They remain approximate guides — treat areas within 5 miles of a polygon edge as potentially affected.
🛩 Military Transit Corridors 6 DOCUMENTED · 15 ESTIMATED

Transit corridors are the most uncertain layer on this map. The 6 documented routes have a traceable published source. The 15 estimated routes are geographic inferences — they represent plausible transit paths but are not confirmed from published documents.

Documented routes (teal, solid)
  • RAF Odiham → Salisbury Plain (Chinook Supply Route) — RAF Odiham Environmental Management Plan; also referenced in Test Valley Borough Council planning consultations.
  • RAF Coningsby → Donna Nook (Typhoon weapons run) — Lincolnshire County Council noise assessments; AIP NOTAMs for Donna Nook Air Weapons Range. Route confirmed in Lincolnshire County Council planning authority records.
  • RAF Coningsby → Holbeach (Typhoon range) — Same noise assessment documentation as above; Holbeach referenced alongside Donna Nook as primary Coningsby weapons range.
  • RAF Marham → Holbeach (F-35 range run) — RAF Marham's published Environmental Information; Norfolk County Council consultation responses for the F-35 transition EIA.
  • RAF Lakenheath → Holbeach (USAF F-15 range run) — Referenced in Suffolk and Norfolk environmental impact assessments for USAF flying operations; Holbeach listed as primary overland range for Lakenheath F-15s.
  • RAF Coningsby → Spadeadam (EW tactics route) — Cumbria County Council planning records for Spadeadam EW Range; referenced in MOD Environmental Statement for range expansion.
Estimated routes (grey, dashed)
15 routes inferred from known origin/destination pairs (airfield to training zone) where no published route description was found. Routes follow the most direct geographic path, offset to avoid major urban centres where an operational reason to do so exists. These routes may be wrong. Actual routes depend on ATC instructions, NOTAMs, airspace restrictions, and standing orders not publicly available.
Corridor width calculation
Widths reflect realistic navigation tolerance for each aircraft type: fast jets (Typhoon, F-35, F-15) — 5–6 miles either side of centreline; heavy helicopters (Chinook) — 4 miles; light helicopters (Puma, Merlin) — 3 miles. Polygons are computed using a perpendicular offset algorithm: for each waypoint, the bearing is averaged from adjacent segments, then a lateral offset point is computed at 90° to that bearing at the specified half-width.
📊 Flyover Rate Estimates ESTIMATED
What is shown
An estimate of how many low-level aircraft movements per day a property might experience, based on its proximity to airfields and how much activity those airfields generate. Shown on each property card with a ℹ popup giving the calculation detail.
Primary data source
CAA UK Airport Data — Table 03.1 (annual aircraft movements by airport, 2025 edition). For smaller airfields not in CAA statistics, OurAirports community data is used with a conservative estimated annual movement rate of 500–2,000 sorties/year.
Interpolation method
For each property, the tool identifies all airfields within a 20-mile radius. Each airfield's annual movement count is divided by 365 to get a daily rate, then weighted by inverse-square of the distance to the property (i.e. an airfield twice as far contributes one-quarter as much). The weighted contributions are summed to produce the displayed figure.

The ℹ popup on each card shows a sentence explaining which specific airfields and reference location were used — for example: "Interpolated using inverse-square distance weighting from: Halesworth reference area, RAF Honington (8.2 mi), Beccles Airfield airstrip (11.4 mi)."
Reference stations
45 named reference locations across England were pre-computed using the same weighted method and stored in flyover-reference.json. Each property is matched to its nearest reference station by name; the reference station's rate is used as the displayed figure, adjusted for the property's own distance profile. This avoids repeating the full calculation for every property at display time.
Accuracy warning
Flyover estimates are indicative only. CAA data includes all movements (commercial, private, military, training) — not exclusively military overflights. Actual noise experience depends on the flight path relative to the property, aircraft type, and day-to-day scheduling. Properties with an ⚠ ESTIMATED banner have imprecise geocoding — the underlying distance calculations may be off by up to several miles, making the interpolated figure correspondingly less reliable.
📍 Property Geocoding ADDRESS · POSTCODE · AREA
Primary source
Postcodes.io — open-source UK postcode API backed by ONS data. Used for postcode-level geocoding when a full address geocode is unavailable.
Geocoding levels
  • Address — precise coordinates from full address lookup. All distance calculations are accurate.
  • Postcode — centroid of the postcode area. May be up to ~1 mile from the actual property. Flagged with an amber ⚠ ESTIMATED banner.
  • Area — centroid of a town or region. May be several miles off. Flagged with a stronger warning.

Data was last compiled June–July 2026. Military airspace and training areas change over time — always verify with official sources before making any property decision based on this information. This tool is for indicative research only.
